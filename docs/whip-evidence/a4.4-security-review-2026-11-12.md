# A4.4 security review: sapflow-dtls and SRTP, 12 November 2026

Gate A4.4 in [whip-ingest.md](../whip-ingest.md) asks for an independent
review of `sapflow-dtls` and of SRTP in `sapflow-rtp`, recorded, with every
finding resolved or accepted in writing.

**Status: not met yet.** The findings of rounds 1 to 6 are fixed: round 5
closed round 4's four, round 6 closed round 5's one, and round 7 closed
round 6's one. Round 7 found two more (findings 13 and 14, P2), both in the
DTLS cookie exchange only. The owner then narrowed A4.4's scope on 15
November 2026 (Scope and method, below): the cookie exchange is now an
experimental, off-by-default `hello-verify` feature outside A4.4, and
findings 13 and 14 stay open against it. A4.4 needs a round 8 independent
review of the narrowed scope before it can be met. The owner accepted the
residuals stated below as inherent in writing on 15 November 2026.

## Scope and method

- **Scope decision, 15 November 2026:** after round 7, the owner decided
  ("Gate it, narrow A4.4") that A4.4 covers `sapflow-dtls` as WHIP builds
  it, without the `hello-verify` feature, plus SRTP. The DTLS cookie
  exchange (`Config::hello_verify`, the HelloVerifyRequest) moved behind
  that feature, which is off by default and experimental; without it the
  field, the cookie and its HMAC, the HelloVerifyRequest and its budget
  are not compiled. Reassembly's unordered hello mode, which the exchange
  uses, is still compiled but unreachable without it. WHIP never enabled the
  exchange (`SessionConfig::dtls` is `Config::default()`, and
  `sapflow-server` does not change it), and neither `sapflow-whip` nor
  `sapflow-server` enables the feature. Rounds 1 to 7 reviewed the cookie
  exchange as part of the crate; findings about it from round 7 on are
  recorded against the feature.
- **Code reviewed:** all of `crates/sapflow-dtls`, and
  `crates/sapflow-rtp/src/srtp.rs` with `crates/sapflow-rtp/src/srtp/`
  (`replay.rs`, `transform.rs` and the tests). The reviewer also read the
  key handoff in `crates/sapflow-whip/src/session.rs`.
- **The reviewer:** the reviewer had no access to the authors' design
  discussion or to the project's earlier reviews. It read the source,
  read-only, in a sandbox that could not write files, and did not build or
  run anything. The owner chose this reviewer for A4.4 on 12 November 2026.
- **Each round:** each round reviewed the whole scope against the same
  brief. The brief covered:
  - DTLS handshake correctness;
  - SRTP and SRTCP cryptography;
  - replay and freshness;
  - input validation and resource bounds;
  - side channels;
  - pinned dependencies;
  - test coverage.

  The authors fixed each finding between rounds. Each fix came with a test
  that failed before it.

## Findings and their resolution

| # | Round | Severity | Finding | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | P2 | A connected server answered a renegotiation ClientHello only when its `message_seq` was above the last Finished's. DTLS 1.2 starts each handshake at 0 (RFC 6347 §4.2.2), so a conforming peer's request got no `no_renegotiation` warning. | Fixed in `7ade6cb`. |
| 2 | 2 | P2 | Unauthenticated epoch 0 input could end a handshake for good: a fragment over the size or buffer bound, or a 15-byte fatal alert, even after ChangeCipherSpec. | Fixed in `1141378`. Such fragments and every epoch 0 alert are dropped and counted. |
| 3 | 3 | P1 | A forged complete message could end the handshake, for example a zero-length Certificate or an epoch 0 Finished, even one queued early. Reassembly advanced its sequence before the message was checked. | Fixed in `8919573`. Admission checks by seq, type, epoch and length; transactional release; inadmissible epoch 0 data refused after CertificateVerify. |
| 4 | 3 | P2 | A finite burst of forged fragments could hold the 16 KiB buffer, or a message's four candidate slots, until the deadline. | Fixed in `8919573` for the cases found: room is reclaimed from stalled or younger forged candidates, paced by the retransmission interval. Round 4 found cases it does not cover (findings 7 and 9). |
| 5 | 3 | P2 | A forged fragment overlapping genuine bytes overwrote them. | Fixed in `8919573`: a disagreeing fragment starts its own candidate. Round 4 found a liveness case in that rule (finding 7). |
| 6 | 3 | follow-up | Every epoch 0 refusal is now silent and ends at the caller's deadline, so a WHIP fingerprint mismatch ended as a plain handshake timeout. | `0cb8488`: at the deadline, WHIP ends the session as a DTLS failure with the reason `Server::refused` holds. |
| 7 | 4 | P1 | Two complementary forged candidates, each disagreeing with a different genuine fragment, absorb every genuine retransmission. Reclamation runs only when a new candidate does not fit, so the handshake is held until the deadline. About 200 forged bytes do it. | Fixed in `355e2ab`, closed in round 5. A fragment that only repeats what stalled candidates hold, while another candidate disagrees with it, starts a fresh candidate; nothing is overwritten (finding 5). |
| 8 | 4 | P1 | A CertificateVerify whose signature is not valid DER passes the message parser, is counted as the one verification, and then fails decoding, ending the handshake at once. 30 bytes queued ahead do it, and so can a buggy peer. | Fixed in `004282e`, closed in round 5. The parser decodes the signature (valid DER, `r` and `s` in range) with the decoder verification uses, so such a message is refused without spending the verification. |
| 9 | 4 | P2 | With `max_buffered` set below the default (for example 512 bytes), complete forged messages at a later seq leave too little room for the expected Certificate, and nothing reclaims complete candidates. The default 16 KiB is not affected. | Fixed in `1204189`, closed in round 5. `max_buffered` must be at least `max_message_len` plus 568 bytes, the most complete messages admitted ahead can hold. |
| 10 | 4 | P2 | The first outgoing record mirrors the ClientHello's record sequence number. A ClientHello at the top of the sequence space leaves no room for the server's flights, so the handshake fails after the server has signed. A buggy peer can do it too. | Fixed in `74513ab`, closed in round 5. The mirroring stays (RFC 6347 section 4.2.1 requires it); a first hello within 65536 of the top is refused before anything is signed or sent. |
| 11 | 5 | P2 | Resends the peer triggers and timer resends share one `max_transmissions` budget, and a peer-triggered resend restarts the timer without doubling it; the next timeout fails the handshake once the budget is spent. With the defaults, five repeats of the accepted ClientHello (a prefix of 34 body bytes is enough) at 200 ms spacing spend the six sends by 1 s, and the timer fails the handshake at about 2 s, so a genuine client flight delayed to 3 s, inside WHIP's 10 s deadline, is refused. An attacker who can inject on the selected tuple, or a buggy peer sending rapid duplicates, can do it. | Fixed in `9bea2ac`, closed in round 6. The budget bounds output only. Flight 4 gets a fixed expiry when first sent, the end of the timer-only schedule (63 s with the defaults); once the budget is spent nothing more is sent and the timer waits for the expiry, so the genuine flight can still complete. A peer-triggered resend never moves the timer past it. The final flight and the HelloVerifyRequest flight have no timer, so their budgets end nothing. |
| 12 | 6 | P2 | With the cookie exchange on, a ClientHello with an invalid or empty cookie is answered with a HelloVerifyRequest and then advances the expected `message_seq`, as an accepted hello would. The genuine hello 0 gets HelloVerifyRequest 0 and the client prepares its cookie-bearing hello 1; an injector first sends a copy of the empty-cookie hello as seq 1, which is answered and moves the expected seq to 2. If that extra HelloVerifyRequest is lost, the genuine hello 1 is old before its cookie is checked, does not repeat the injected one (the cookie changes its length), and is ignored until the caller's deadline. It needs an attacker who can inject on the selected tuple and the loss of the extra HelloVerifyRequest; the cookie exchange is off by default, and WHIP never turns it on. No signature, ECDH or verification is spent, so it is not the accepted residual. | Fixed in `ad0d3ee`, closed in round 7. Until a hello has a valid cookie, each hello stands alone, as RFC 6347 section 4.2.1's stateless exchange intends: hellos are taken at any seq in a window that covers every seq a client moved on by each HelloVerifyRequest can reach, a HelloVerifyRequest commits nothing, and the hello with a valid cookie sets the expected seq, the transcript and the server's sequence numbers. A repeat of the hello last answered resends its HelloVerifyRequest within that flight's budget, the budget of answers is unchanged, and nothing is signed before a valid cookie. The same cause let a forged first hello at seq 0, a wrong non-empty cookie, or several injected seqs exclude the genuine hello; each is covered. |
| 13 | 7 | P2 | With the cookie exchange on, only a repeat of the hello last answered counts as a retransmission; every other hello without a valid cookie spends one of the `max_transmissions` answers, which never come back. Six forged empty-cookie hellos sent before the publisher's first (for example at `message_seq` 0 with two alternating randoms) spend them all, and the genuine hello then never gets a HelloVerifyRequest. It needs an attacker who can inject on the selected tuple. The suggested fix is a budget of answers or bytes that recovers over time instead of a lifetime cap. | Open, experimental `hello-verify` feature only, outside A4.4's scope by the owner's decision of 15 November 2026. |
| 14 | 7 | P2 (conformance) | The outgoing epoch 0 record counter is seeded from the ClientHello's record sequence number only while nothing has been sent, so later HelloVerifyRequests, and the first ServerHello after a cookie exchange, do not mirror the record number of the hello that triggered them, as RFC 6347 section 4.2.1 requires. Ordinary loss triggers it. It has not been shown to break a browser. The test `a_retransmitted_first_hello_repeats_one_hello_verify_request` expects today's behaviour. | Open, experimental `hello-verify` feature only, outside A4.4's scope by the owner's decision of 15 November 2026. |

## Residuals stated as inherent

Round 4 judged that DTLS 1.2 cannot give guaranteed availability against:

- a structurally valid substituted ClientHello, a valid P-256 key share, or
  a well-formed but wrong CertificateVerify signature, any of which arrives
  before the genuine message. It spends the one signature, ECDH or
  verification the server allows per handshake, and fails it. The genuine
  message cannot be told apart until the transcript is authenticated, and
  retries would change that bound without guaranteeing recovery;
- sustained injection, which can keep refilling the buffer and slots.

Round 4 did not accept the broader "a finite burst cannot hold a handshake"
as inherent: findings 7, 9 and the parser part of 8 have bounded fixes.
**Owner acceptance of these residuals: given in writing on 15 November
2026** ("Accept them"). It covers these two residuals only. Findings 7 to 12
are not residuals: 7 to 10 are fixed and closed in round 5, 11 is fixed and
closed in round 6, and 12 is fixed and closed in round 7. Findings 13 and
14 are not residuals either: they are open in the experimental
`hello-verify` feature, outside A4.4.

## Checked and found sound

Across the rounds, on source inspection:

- **Handshake authentication:**
  - the extended master secret is required, and the transcript is built
    correctly;
  - the P-256 suite and points are validated;
  - the certificate fingerprint is checked against the SDP offer before
    the DER is parsed, in constant time, against every expected
    fingerprint;
  - the CertificateVerify proof and the constant-time Finished comparison
    hold;
  - exported keys are withheld until authentication completes.
- **Negotiation:**
  - the SRTP profile is chosen by preference;
  - a non-empty MKI is refused;
  - a DTLS 1.3-capable offer is handled correctly;
  - the RFC 5705 exporter's label, random ordering and absent context are
    right, and so is the split into WHIP's server-role keys (client keys
    inbound, server keys outbound).
- **Records:**
  - replay-window updates happen only after authentication;
  - retransmissions use fresh record sequence numbers and nonces;
  - sequence exhaustion is checked;
  - flights, timers and queues are bounded;
  - cookie secrets are per association.
- **SRTP and SRTCP:**
  - the RFC 3711 derivation labels and AES-CM IVs, and HMAC-SHA1-80;
  - the AES-GCM nonces, AAD and tag placement;
  - ROC estimation and wrap;
  - SRTCP's E bit and index exhaustion;
  - replay-state updates only after authentication;
  - the 96-bit GCM master salt, which is padded on the right as RFC 7714
    erratum 4938 requires.
- **Comparisons and errors:** MAC, tag, fingerprint and Finished
  comparisons are constant time, and no secret-revealing error oracle was
  found.
- **Dependencies:**
  - the direct crypto dependencies are pinned exactly: `aes-gcm` 0.11.1,
    `aes` 0.9.3, `p256` 0.14.0, `ecdsa` 0.17.0, `hmac` 0.13.0, `sha1` 0.11.0,
    `sha2` 0.11.1 and `subtle` 2.6.1;
  - the locked `crypto-bigint`, `ctutils`, `cmov` and `aes-gcm` versions are
    outside the affected ranges of the advisories checked;
  - the `testing` feature exposes deterministic helpers but does not
    replace production randomness or disable fingerprint checks.
