# A4.4 security review: sapflow-dtls and SRTP, 12 November 2026

Gate A4.4 in [whip-ingest.md](../whip-ingest.md) asks for an independent
review of `sapflow-dtls` and of SRTP in `sapflow-rtp`, recorded, with every
finding resolved or accepted in writing.

**Status: not met yet.** The findings of rounds 1 to 3 are fixed. Round 4's
four findings are open. The residuals stated below as inherent await the
owner's written acceptance.

## Scope and method

- **Code reviewed:** all of `crates/sapflow-dtls`, and
  `crates/sapflow-rtp/src/srtp.rs` with `crates/sapflow-rtp/src/srtp/`
  (`replay.rs`, `transform.rs` and the tests). The reviewer also read the
  key handoff in `crates/sapflow-whip/src/session.rs`.
- **The reviewer:** the reviewer had no access to the authors' design
  discussion or to the project's earlier reviews. It read the source,
  read-only, in a sandbox that could not write files, and did not build or
  run anything. The owner chose this reviewer for A4.4 on 12 November 2026.
- **Each round:** each round reviewed the whole scope against the same
  brief. The brief covered:
  - DTLS handshake correctness;
  - SRTP and SRTCP cryptography;
  - replay and freshness;
  - input validation and resource bounds;
  - side channels;
  - pinned dependencies;
  - test coverage.

  The authors fixed each finding between rounds. Each fix came with a test
  that failed before it.

handshake completes, plus one renegotiation refusal.

## Findings and their resolution

| # | Round | Severity | Finding | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | P2 | A connected server answered a renegotiation ClientHello only when its `message_seq` was above the last Finished's. DTLS 1.2 starts each handshake at 0 (RFC 6347 §4.2.2), so a conforming peer's request got no `no_renegotiation` warning. | Fixed in `7ade6cb`. |
| 2 | 2 | P2 | Unauthenticated epoch 0 input could end a handshake for good: a fragment over the size or buffer bound, or a 15-byte fatal alert, even after ChangeCipherSpec. | Fixed in `1141378`. Such fragments and every epoch 0 alert are dropped and counted. |
| 3 | 3 | P1 | A forged complete message could end the handshake, for example a zero-length Certificate or an epoch 0 Finished, even one queued early. Reassembly advanced its sequence before the message was checked. | Fixed in `8919573`. Admission checks by seq, type, epoch and length; transactional release; inadmissible epoch 0 data refused after CertificateVerify. |
| 4 | 3 | P2 | A finite burst of forged fragments could hold the 16 KiB buffer, or a message's four candidate slots, until the deadline. | Fixed in `8919573` for the cases found: room is reclaimed from stalled or younger forged candidates, paced by the retransmission interval. Round 4 found cases it does not cover (findings 7 and 9). |
| 5 | 3 | P2 | A forged fragment overlapping genuine bytes overwrote them. | Fixed in `8919573`: a disagreeing fragment starts its own candidate. Round 4 found a liveness case in that rule (finding 7). |
| 6 | 3 | follow-up | Every epoch 0 refusal is now silent and ends at the caller's deadline, so a WHIP fingerprint mismatch ended as a plain handshake timeout. | `0cb8488`: at the deadline, WHIP ends the session as a DTLS failure with the reason `Server::refused` holds. |
| 7 | 4 | P1 | Two complementary forged candidates, each disagreeing with a different genuine fragment, absorb every genuine retransmission. Reclamation runs only when a new candidate does not fit, so the handshake is held until the deadline. About 200 forged bytes do it. | **Open.** |
| 8 | 4 | P1 | A CertificateVerify whose signature is not valid DER passes the message parser, is counted as the one verification, and then fails decoding, ending the handshake at once. 30 bytes queued ahead do it, and so can a buggy peer. | **Open.** |
| 9 | 4 | P2 | With `max_buffered` set below the default (for example 512 bytes), complete forged messages at a later seq leave too little room for the expected Certificate, and nothing reclaims complete candidates. The default 16 KiB is not affected. | **Open.** |
| 10 | 4 | P2 | The first outgoing record mirrors the ClientHello's record sequence number. A ClientHello at the top of the sequence space leaves no room for the server's flights, so the handshake fails after the server has signed. A buggy peer can do it too. | **Open.** |

Findings 7 to 10 were not fixed in this round of work. The owner decided on
12 November 2026 to merge the fixes for rounds 1 to 3 and record round 4's
findings as open. Except where a buggy peer can trigger one (findings 8 and
10), each needs an attacker who can spoof the publisher's selected address
pair during the handshake. WHIP is off by default and does not face the
internet before the W4 gates pass.

## Residuals stated as inherent

Round 4 judged that DTLS 1.2 cannot give guaranteed availability against:

- a structurally valid substituted ClientHello, a valid P-256 key share, or
  a well-formed but wrong CertificateVerify signature, any of which arrives
  before the genuine message. It spends the one signature, ECDH or
  verification the server allows per handshake, and fails it. The genuine
  message cannot be told apart until the transcript is authenticated, and
  retries would change that bound without guaranteeing recovery;
- sustained injection, which can keep refilling the buffer and slots.

Round 4 did not accept the broader "a finite burst cannot hold a handshake"
as inherent: findings 7, 9 and the parser part of 8 have bounded fixes.
**Owner acceptance of these residuals: pending.**

## Checked and found sound

Across the rounds, on source inspection:

- **Handshake authentication:**
  - the extended master secret is required, and the transcript is built
    correctly;
  - the P-256 suite and points are validated;
  - the certificate fingerprint is checked against the SDP offer before
    the DER is parsed, in constant time, against every expected
    fingerprint;
  - the CertificateVerify proof and the constant-time Finished comparison
    hold;
  - exported keys are withheld until authentication completes.
- **Negotiation:**
  - the SRTP profile is chosen by preference;
  - a non-empty MKI is refused;
  - a DTLS 1.3-capable offer is handled correctly;
  - the RFC 5705 exporter's label, random ordering and absent context are
    right, and so is the split into WHIP's server-role keys (client keys
    inbound, server keys outbound).
- **Records:**
  - replay-window updates happen only after authentication;
  - retransmissions use fresh record sequence numbers and nonces;
  - sequence exhaustion is checked;
  - flights, timers and queues are bounded;
  - cookie secrets are per association.
- **SRTP and SRTCP:**
  - the RFC 3711 derivation labels and AES-CM IVs, and HMAC-SHA1-80;
  - the AES-GCM nonces, AAD and tag placement;
  - ROC estimation and wrap;
  - SRTCP's E bit and index exhaustion;
  - replay-state updates only after authentication;
  - the 96-bit GCM master salt, which is padded on the right as RFC 7714
    erratum 4938 requires.
- **Comparisons and errors:** MAC, tag, fingerprint and Finished
  comparisons are constant time, and no secret-revealing error oracle was
  found.
- **Dependencies:**
  - the direct crypto dependencies are pinned exactly: `aes-gcm` 0.11.1,
    `aes` 0.9.3, `p256` 0.14.0, `ecdsa` 0.17.0, `hmac` 0.13.0, `sha1` 0.11.0,
    `sha2` 0.11.1 and `subtle` 2.6.1;
  - the locked `crypto-bigint`, `ctutils`, `cmov` and `aes-gcm` versions are
    outside the affected ranges of the advisories checked;
  - the `testing` feature exposes deterministic helpers but does not
    replace production randomness or disable fingerprint checks.

## Not checked by the review

- **Builds and runs:** builds, tests, fuzzing and live interoperability.
  The authors ran these for each fix:
  - the crate gate;
  - `dtls_server` and `whip_session` fuzzing for 600 s each;
  - OpenSSL 4 interoperability, 8 of 8 matching;
  - headless Chrome 157 over UDP and ICE-TCP.
- **Clients not tested now:** current Safari, Firefox and OBS behaviour.
- **Timing and audits:** compiled-code timing on deployment CPUs, an audit of
  the curve arithmetic, which the `p256` crate states is not independently
  audited, and a complete automated advisory scan.
- **Deployment:** the deployed signalling and admission configuration.
